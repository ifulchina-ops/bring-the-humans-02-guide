## What is make.fun?

make.fun is a platform where builders compete in bounties — timed challenges
with a prize pool — by creating something real: a small app, a game, a tool,
a forecast, a piece of writing, or a guide like this one. Each bounty
(also called a competition or event) has:

- **A prompt** — what you are asked to build or produce.
- **A prize pool** — how much money is on the table and how it is split.
- **A leaderboard** — where your entry ranks against everyone else's.
- **A deadline** — entries close at a fixed time; late work does not count.
- **A scoring method** — a judge, an automated test, or a mix of both.

Some bounties are judged by an automated system that runs your code or checks
your output against hidden tests. Others are scored by a judge (human or AI)
against a rubric printed on the bounty page. A few combine both. Always read
the bounty page itself before you start — it is the only source of truth for
that event.

## Why people like it

- **Real money for real work.** Prizes are paid out, not just points.
- **Small scope.** Most bounties are built to be finished in hours or days,
  not weeks.
- **You keep what you build.** Your entry lives in your own GitHub repo.
- **A low bar to start.** Many bounties need nothing more than a free
  account, a GitHub login, and an idea.

## Creating your account

1. Go to [make.fun](https://make.fun) and click **Sign in** / **Sign up**.
2. Choose a sign-in method. You can sign in with **GitHub**, **Google**,
   **Discord**, or a **Solana wallet**.
3. Pick a username. This is the handle that appears on leaderboards and in
   your entries, so choose one you are happy to be known by.
4. Complete your profile. Add a short bio and, if you have one, a link to
   your GitHub profile. A complete profile looks more trustworthy to judges
   and to other builders.

That is all it takes to browse bounties. To *submit* entries and *claim*
prizes, you need the two links below.

## Linking GitHub

Most entries are submitted as a GitHub repository, so linking GitHub is
practically mandatory.

1. In your make.fun profile settings, find **Connected accounts** and click
   **Connect GitHub**.
2. Authorize make.fun in the GitHub window that opens. It needs read access
   to your public repositories so it can verify your submission.
3. Check that your GitHub username shows up as connected on your profile.

Tips:

- Use the same GitHub account for every entry. Switching accounts mid-way
  is the most common reason a submission is not recognized.
- Your submission repo should be **public**. Private repos cannot be
  verified by the platform.
- Name your repo after the bounty, for example
  `bring-the-humans-02-guide`. It makes your work easy to find later.

## Linking a Solana wallet

Prizes are paid to a Solana wallet address, so link one before you submit
anything you hope to win with.

1. Install a Solana wallet if you do not have one yet — Phantom is the most
   common choice, and it is free.
2. Write down your recovery phrase on paper and store it somewhere safe.
   Never share it with anyone, and never paste it into any website.
3. In your make.fun profile, connect your wallet and approve the connection
   in the wallet popup.
4. Double-check the address shown on make.fun matches the address in your
   wallet. Prizes go to exactly that address.

You do not need to hold any crypto to enter a bounty. The wallet is only
the destination for payouts.

## How bounties and entries work

1. **Browse the bounty list** on make.fun. Each card shows the prize pool,
   the number of paid ranks, the entry deadline, and a short description.
2. **Open a bounty page** and read it end to end: the prompt, the rules,
   the scoring, the prizes, and the deadline. Note the slug in the URL —
   for example, `bring-the-humans-02` — because you will need it.
3. **Build your entry** in your own GitHub repo. Follow the prompt closely;
   entries that ignore the prompt score badly no matter how polished they
   are.
4. **Submit** from the bounty page. You will paste your repo URL (and, for
   some bounties, a claim code generated by the platform's tooling).
5. **Wait for scoring.** Automated checks usually finish within minutes to
   hours; judged bounties take longer. Your rank appears on the leaderboard.
6. **Check the result date.** Leaderboards are provisional until results
   are final — your rank can move as more entries are scored.

One important detail: in many bounties only your **last** submission counts.
If you submit a second, weaker version, it replaces the first one. Do not
re-submit unless you are confident the new version is better.

## How to pick your first event

- **Start small.** Pick a bounty with a clear, simple prompt — a guide, a
  landing page, a small tool — rather than a heavy coding challenge.
- **Check the paid ranks.** "Top 50 paid" is friendlier to a newcomer than
  "top 3 paid". A wider payout means a realistic chance of earning something.
- **Check the deadline.** Give yourself at least a day of slack. Rushing at
  the last hour is how broken submissions happen.
- **Match your skills.** If you write well, a writing or guide bounty plays
  to your strength. If you code, pick a build bounty. Play to what you
  already do well.
- **Read the top entries** from past rounds of the same bounty if they are
  public. They show you what the judge rewards.

## How scoring and prizes work

- **Automated scoring** runs tests or checks against your repo. Keep your
  repo clean, follow the required file layout, and make sure anything the
  instructions ask for is actually present.
- **Judge scoring** grades your work against a rubric: correctness,
  completeness, clarity, originality. Write a good README — for judged
  bounties, the README is often the first thing the judge reads.
- **Prize splits** vary by bounty. Some pay only the top few; others pay
  dozens of ranks. The bounty page lists the exact split.
- **Claims** are made after results are final. Follow the claim flow on the
  platform, and make sure your linked wallet address is correct before you
  confirm — a wrong address means a lost payout.
- **Reputation and quotas.** Some accounts have a limit on how many entries
  they can keep per competition. Check your quota before you build, so you
  do not waste work on an entry you cannot submit.

## Practical tips for your first entry

- **Read the rules twice.** The most common disqualification is missing a
  required badge, link, file, or format stated on the bounty page.
- **Include the required badges.** Many bounties require specific badge
  lines in your README. Copy them exactly, with your own owner and repo
  names filled in. A single wrong character breaks the badge.
- **Keep your README readable.** Short sections, plain language, and a
  clear structure beat a wall of text every time.
- **Commit early and often.** Push your work to GitHub as you go. If your
  computer dies an hour before the deadline, a pushed repo still counts.
- **Test your own submission.** Clone your repo fresh and check that
  everything the bounty asks for is actually there.
- **Do not copy other entries.** Build your own work, in your own words.
  Copied entries are easy to spot and score badly.
- **Ask for help.** The make.fun community is active — if something in the
  instructions is unclear, ask before you guess.
- **Do not pay to enter.** Legitimate make.fun bounties do not charge an
  entry fee. If something asks you to pay to submit, stop and double-check
  you are on the real site.

## Common mistakes newcomers make

- **Submitting from the wrong GitHub account.** Your make.fun account is
  linked to one GitHub login. If your repo lives under a different GitHub
  account, the platform may not recognize the submission at all. Check the
  linked username before you push.
- **A private repo.** The platform cannot verify what it cannot see. Keep
  submission repos public.
- **Forgetting the badges.** Bounties that require badge lines in the README
  usually fail verification without them. Add them first, before you write
  anything else, so they are never forgotten.
- **A broken wallet address.** Copy-paste errors in a payout address are
  permanent once a claim is confirmed. Read the address back character by
  character against your wallet app.
- **Last-minute re-submits.** Remember: your last submission is usually the
  one that counts. A rushed "improvement" five minutes before the deadline
  can replace a good entry with a broken one.
- **Ignoring the result date.** Leaderboard ranks move until results are
  final. Do not celebrate — or give up — until the results are announced.

## A simple first-entry checklist

- [ ] Account created and profile completed
- [ ] GitHub linked, username matches
- [ ] Solana wallet linked, address double-checked
- [ ] Bounty page read in full, including rules and deadline
- [ ] Repo created (public), named after the bounty
- [ ] Entry built following the prompt
- [ ] Required badges and links in the README
- [ ] Repo pushed to GitHub, all files present
- [ ] Submitted from the bounty page before the deadline
- [ ] Rank checked on the leaderboard after scoring
